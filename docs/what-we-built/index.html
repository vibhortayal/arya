Built in the hackathon window

The hackathon window opened on Oct 27, 2026. This page says what existed before it, what I made in it and when, taken from git. Times are US Eastern. What git cannot show is listed under "What git cannot confirm".

What existed before the window​

  • The idea search. By my own account it took about two days. The commits for it are in a separate research repo (vibhortayal/meta-hackathon-research). They start on Oct 26 at 12:37. The 28 commits that day, up to 16:51, hold candidate ideas and a plan for spikes, with no spike results. This is before Oct 27, and I say so plainly.
  • My 114 Costco receipts (back to 2023) are my own purchase history, imported as data. They are not in this repo.
  • The server. The Oracle VM already existed for my other hobby projects. Setting up Arya on it began on Oct 27.
  • Nothing in the Arya repo is older than Oct 27, 15:13 ET. Its first commit already holds the design documents (SPEC.md and IMPLEMENTATION.md, about 900 lines), which I wrote before committing them.

What exists today​

The 3-minute video cannot show everything. This list is the standout parts of Arya that exist today, each with why it matters and where the detail lives. Everything here is built and in the code. Ideas that are not built are on the After the hackathon page. For the product in full, see assistant/docs/SPEC.md and assistant/docs/USER-JOURNEYS.md.

Receipts you can trust​

  • A model reads, code checks. Muse Spark reads a receipt photo first (Wonder is the backup reader). Plain code then checks every line against the printed total before anything posts. Why: a wrong price never goes in silently. assistant/docs/MODEL-POLICY.md, docs/evals/
  • Returns net out against purchases.
  • "To check" marks lines Arya is unsure about. Fix this line edits the name, quantity and price, with Undo. The printed total never changes. The original photo is kept. Why: you stay in control of your record.
  • Background reading with progress: several receipts at once, and you can leave while Arya reads them.

A list that learns​

  • Patterns from your own receipts: how often you buy each item and what you pay at each store. From that, the list shows what is running low and what to use soon, each with a reason (the "why" sheet).
  • A staple judge with two seats: Muse Spark decides what counts as a staple. Muse Spark Open (open weights, hosted on Baseten) judges again from item names only. Disagreements are kept for review. Why: names advise, purchase facts decide. assistant/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md
  • The Curator proposes product merges and splits with reasons; nothing changes until you apply it.
  • Your own lists, and every item sits on one: you make your own lists, for example, one for each store, such as Trader Jill's or Costcozy. When you add an item, it goes to the list you say, or to your only list, or Arya asks "Which list?".
  • Works offline (signed-in accounts): lists and other screens show as last saved, and list edits queue until you are back online. Receipts, Ask, photos, voice and sharing need a connection. Signed-in accounts keep a cached copy. The demo saves offline list changes for that visit only.

Prices from your own receipts​

  • Cheaper store, price trend, similar items per ounce, which store to buy each item at, and the cost of food waste, on a Spending screen. Why: nothing is fetched from store sites, flyers or the web, so every figure is yours. assistant/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md
  • Community prices and live "how busy is the store" are not live; the opt-in records a choice only (SPEC.md, FR-11.7).

Ask Arya​

  • A chat over a snapshot of your household's data that can also act on statements such as "we finished the yogurt".
  • Answers in the language you write; Spanish and Hindi were the ones verified (assistant/docs/MULTILINGUAL-ASK.md).
  • Voice dictation and a photo of a handwritten list.

Quest, the optional game​

  • A hunt through a model of your store with no clock and no way to lose. Start shopping on a list opens it at that list's store and ends on your receipt. docs/quest/GAME-DESIGN.md
  • Find it for me: point the camera at a shelf; SAM 3.1 boxes candidates and Muse Spark checks each one.
  • Map my store: a guided camera scan. SAM 3.1 pixelates the people it detects before anything is kept. If that fails, the frame is discarded. Detection is not perfect and is not scored. A store counts as mapped only where it has a usable, fresh photo, so the status is earned. docs/quest/MAP-MY-STORE.md
  • Build a store from three questions, practice in it, and save it.

Groups and everyday use​

  • Groups share lists between the people you live with, joined by a single-use code or an invite link. Receipts stay with whoever filed them.
  • Daily reminders for what is running low. Live on the hosted app. You opt in, and on iPhone the app must be added to the Home Screen first.
  • Add to Home Screen, so it opens like an app.
  • A navigation tab (the bottom buttons) you arrange yourself.

Transparency about AI and cost​

  • Keeping Arya running (Settings, About Arya) shows your own AI use by kind (receipts and photos, Ask, store maps, lists and voice), this month and since you joined, and the whole app's AI cost and who pays. Why: Arya is free, and the paid AI reading is shown openly.
  • How Arya uses AI says where AI is used and when to double-check, and that Arya is not a food-safety check.
  • Arya's powers lists what Arya does for you now and what turns on as you add receipts.
  • Guards: a daily budget for the real app and a separate one for the demo, a per-visit cap on the demo, and a heads-up and pause for unusually heavy use. assistant/docs/OPS.md

Open, and bring your own tools​

  • Lists API and developer portal at /developers (quickstart in curl, Python and JavaScript, interactive reference). docs/API.md
  • A connector for your own AI agent (MCP): Make my code, then ask the Muse agent to connect. assistant/docs/AGENT-CONNECTOR.md

Built to run​

  • One image, also run locally with Docker in a few steps (docs/RUN-IT-YOURSELF.md).
  • A /health check that shows the running commit, nightly backups, and an owner-only Ops screen with a daily report, a pause switch for paid reads, spend ceilings and a cost ledger with one row per model call.
  • Tests and measured results: STATUS.md, docs/evals/.

In the code but switched off​

  • Use your own Meta key (unfinished, not used). A Settings row, a Help topic, an Ask answer and a sign-up door. An account with its own key would never fall back to Arya's keys. I decided not to use it, so it is switched off and not part of the demo. After the hackathon
  • Alexa skill (built, switched off, not tested on a real device). An Alexa skill that adds items to your Arya list by voice. The code is merged (#515, #516) behind the ARYA_ALEXA flag, which is off. I paused it until after the hackathon, and it has not been tried on a real Echo. It is voice add only. Amazon has retired its Alexa list API, so Arya cannot sync with Alexa's own lists.
