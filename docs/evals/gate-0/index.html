Gate 0: public photo-receipt fixture set, report

Date: 2026-10-27. Model: muse-spark-1.3 (Standard), temperature 0.1, SYSTEM prompt taken verbatim from spike2/decode_photo.py. Labels were written by hand from the photos BEFORE any decode ran. Each decode call was stateless.

Verdict​

SetLines correct / labeledAccuracyvs 90% gate
Public set (identity metric)90 / 9891.84%PASS
Public set (strict-string metric)80 / 9881.63%FAIL
Combined: my subset (32/32) + public (identity)122 / 13093.85%PASS
Combined (strict-string)112 / 13086.15%FAIL

Two metrics are reported because the task rule ("product identity matches the printed identity, allowing spacing/case differences only") and the decode prompt pull in opposite directions. The SYSTEM prompt itself tells the model to expand brand abbreviations when confident (GG = Green Giant is its own example). The identity metric counts a line correct when the decoded product is the same product as printed (abbreviation expansions such as Bh to Boar's Head, BC to Betty Crocker, KS to Kirkland Signature, and word reordering from the brand/product/detail field split are accepted) and the paid price is exact. The strict-string metric accepts only spacing and case differences. All 10 lines that separate the two metrics are abbreviation expansions, and nothing else. Recommendation: judge the gate on the identity metric. A strict-string gate would penalize behavior the prompt orders. Note: if my subset's 32/32 was scored strict-string, the two subsets are not comparable, and the combined strict figure (86.15%) is the conservative reading.

Isolation compliance​

  • All public-set data lives ONLY under ~/workspace/meta-hackathon-2026/spike2/public-set/ (photos/, labels/, results/, plus decode_public.py, score_public.py, and this report).
  • The only files on my side read were spike2/decode_photo.py and spike2/run_one.py (to reuse the exact SYSTEM prompt and call pattern) and my subset's result line in spike2/SPIKE2-REPORT.md (4 receipts, 32/32) for the combined figure.
  • Nothing was written to spike1/, spike2/photos/, or spike2/results/. No public item entered any dictionary, ledger, or pattern file. No shared state exists between calls.

Sourcing shortfall (stated plainly)​

Target was 16 public receipts; 9 were obtained (98 labelable lines). The limit was whether the lines could be labeled by hand, not search effort (about 25 image searches plus dataset, archive, and blog sweeps). Real receipts that were found but rejected because the labels could not be trusted:

  • Trader Joe's, 26 items, $52.18: the holder's thumb and a fold cut off the left edge of nearly every product name. Only about 4 lines could be labeled as printed.
  • Fred Meyer (Kroger banner), 15 items, $60.69: photographed at an angle. The item text was too small to transcribe reliably after rotation and upscaling.
  • Target, ~7 lines, about $123.90: skewed photo, item names not reliably readable.
  • Costco (Roseville) and a second Costco photo: thumbs hide the product names.
  • CVS/Walgreens photos found were coupon tails only (no item lines); one WIC slip showed quantities without prices and part of a card number (skipped on both grounds).
  • Rejected outright: fake/AI sample receipts, rendered samples, non-US receipts (Canada, UK, Serbia, Russia, Australia, Colombia), staged prop receipts, and photos too blurry to label.

Mix targets. Long receipts (15+ lines): target 4, got 1 (Alexander's, 27 lines); the other long candidates above could not be labeled. Chains beyond Safeway: target 3, got 8. Adverse conditions: hand-held (pub-01, 03, 08, 09), crumpled and rotated 90° (pub-02, kept in original orientation), faded (pub-04, 06), dark and thumb-occluded (pub-08, 09): covered.

Errors, ranked​

  1. Product wrong: 5 (all pub-04, faded vintage print). Digit and code misreads inside abbreviated strings: CHRY48 read as CHRY8, 8PK as SPK, 1884 as 18&4, T/HOUSE16BN as 168N, 96ZPP499 as 962PP499. Four of five carried needs_user_check. All five prices were exact.
  2. Price wrong: 3 (all pub-05), a three-way price rotation: ORANGE JUICE 8.99 read as 3.99, NAPOLITANER 2.99 as 4.05, PIZZA POCKETS 4.05 as 7.99. The rotation keeps the line sum, so the arithmetic check alone would not catch it. The ORANGE JUICE error was high-confidence (0.9, no check flag). It is the one silent error in the set.
  3. Item-number wrong: 3 of 46 checked (reported separately, not in the pass mark): Walmart RED BELL …4688KF read as …4680KF; Westside BOUNTY …81539 as …81599, and NAPOLITANER 0765600721 as 07858000721.
  4. Missed lines: 0. Extra lines: 0. The model emitted exactly the label line count on all 9 receipts, including the 10 label-unreadable lines on the two partial receipts, which it returned as partial reads with needs_user_check (excluded from scoring, not counted as extra).

Arithmetic match: 7/7 receipts where a total is in frame (decoded line sum within 0.05 of the subtotal, and decoded total equal to the printed total). pub-08/pub-09 are not scorable (totals out of frame); on both, the model reported the visible-line sum as its total.

Conventions and notes:

  • Savings folds were handled correctly everywhere they occur: pub-02 (0.50, 0.68), pub-03 (25%-off line: list 9.32 / paid 6.99 / discount 2.33), pub-09 (Costco instant savings folded into CRNCHMASTER: paid 5.99, discount 2.00). The pub-09 label records the printed line amount (7.99); the line is scored correct under the established spike-1/spike-2 fold convention, with the 2.00 carried in the discount field.
  • items_sold matched the label on all 3 receipts that print it (pub-01: 14, pub-03: 12, pub-06: 3). Where not printed, the model inferred a count (pub-02: 10, pub-04: 27, pub-05: 14) instead of returning null. Harmless here, but worth a prompt note.
  • pub-07: the model duplicated the brand token into the product field (a schema artifact); identity was unaffected under token-set comparison.
  • Decode cost: 72,344 tokens total for 9 photos (23,379 prompt + 48,965 completion).
