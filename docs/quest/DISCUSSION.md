# Discussion log — how Arya Quest got its shape

The owner's steering, in order, 2026-10-27. Each entry ends with what it changed.

1. **A 3D store, for fun.** "Build a 3D model of the grocery store from pictures and videos I take — walk through it, it shows you we are here, go next, go next." → The store layer stopped being a camera-overlay afterthought and became a place you walk. (Stage 5 reframed.)
2. **Compliance check.** "Does it comply with the hackathon guidelines and use Meta products?" → Rule set: the 3D is the window, Meta models are the builder. Rendering/plumbing may be commodity; perception, reconstruction, and judgment must be Meta.
3. **A Meta model for the 3D itself.** Owner asked for a Meta product to build 3D from video; he supplies walkthrough videos (a Trader Joe's tour first). → **VGGT / VGGT-Ω** (Meta AI + Oxford) does geometry; SAM 3.1 does products; Spark does labels. Blender demoted to optional cleanup. Spike: 90 frames from a 90-second HD aisle stretch, reconstruction run same day.
4. **The pivot: make shopping fun.** List in any form (handwritten note photo, whiteboard, paste) → pick the store → unmapped stores get mapped by their own shoppers filming aisles → next visit, you *play* the shop. Game skins over one mechanic: treasure hunt, escape room. → The product's face became the game; Arya's engine (patterns, receipts, ledger) moved underneath as the brain.
5. **The receipt ends every shop.** "At the end we always capture the receipt — that feeds our eventual goal." → The receipt is the finish ritual, the scorecard, and the verified data source in one act.
6. **Platform vs account.** Modeling and store uploads live at **platform level**, shared by all players of a store. Lists, receipts, parsing, and personal analysis live at **account level**, private. → The two-plane architecture in ARCHITECTURE.md.
7. **The Pokémon GO frame.** Catching characters in the real world is the engagement model — personal spawns, viral shape. → Pitch's one-liner: "Pokémon GO for your grocery list."
8. **Kids are the sharp end.** Kids cry and scatter in stores; a hunting kid follows the list exactly. → Parents-first audience; the family case is a design center, not an edge case.
9. **Modes are moods, not ages.** "Sometimes you are in a rush, sometimes in the fun mood — don't call it kids mode and parent mode." → **Quest mode** (play) and **Express mode** (rush), same model and list.
10. **Wireframes before building.** Easy, intuitive, simple. His words: tap → the list appears; check things off → the list goes away; multiple stores in a day = different games, sessions on the side. → UX.md (session model, five list-panel rules) + a 10-screen clickable wireframe browser. **Approved by the owner the same evening** — the build gate is open.
11. **Community layer.** Leaderboards, funny verified badges, teams racing live, game-show clue challenges. → GAME-DESIGN.md "Community layer": receipt-verified boards, the badge catalog (Egg Baron … Return Regret), Shop-Offs, Split the List, Clue Hunt. Docs and pitch now; score-screen badges in the demo; full build post-hackathon.

12. **Shop-Off ships.** "Shop-off needs to be there — the community section is the clincher for hackathon virality." → Community promoted from rollout to demo-grade build scope: playable Shop-Off, household board, live badges.
13. **Friends and Profile ship.** "Add friends, profile — this needs to be there." → Handle-based friends with challenge shortcuts; profile as the public card (stats, badge shelf, mapper credit; private data never shown). Wireframe screens 12–13.
14. **Muse writes the list.** "Need to have Muse connector to put items on grocery list too." → The connector (already live on the engine) becomes session-aware: chat adds land in today's session for the named store. Fifth intake door, added to the wireframes' Add-list screen.

15. **Profile holds the receipts.** "Profile can save receipt photos and have option to see digitized the list as well. Future part is analysis dashboard etc." → Profile = public card + private receipt archive (photo with digitized twin); analysis/dashboard declared as the future layer, shown in the UI as a named placeholder only.

16. **A smart optimizer is step one.** "If I add milk three times it should update the quantity… if I put a spelling mistake it should correct it or ask me… I might buy organic blueberries AND frozen blueberries — it should reason it out, or ask me." → The Optimizer (ARCHITECTURE §2b): merge → correct (shown, with undo) → ask on real ambiguity; runs after store pick, before mode choose, on lists from all five doors; unanswered questions block the start.
17. **Drift patrol, paid on truth.** "We also need a way for users to capture drift in store design… Users should be rewarded for this?" — and the stockout objection, raised by the owner himself: a missing item may be moved, or merely out of stock. → The Spotter economy (GAME-DESIGN.md): "Not here" with Empty shelf / Something else here / Can't tell + a shelf photo (the tag is the tell); reports flag, time resolves (a later collect at the modeled stop = stockout; an independent confirm at a new spot = the move applies); points land only on resolution — 2 for a true stockout, 10 for a verified move — plus the Eagle Eye badge and per-store Guardian credit, all status, never cash. Owner's capstone: "This builds real-time community-driven inventory!" — every hunt is a shelf survey, so the platform knows what a store holds right now, and stockouts feed pre-trip warnings.
18. **Legal watchlist.** "Is there any legal pitfalls I should be aware of for this?" → a watchlist was written (filming on private property, bystanders in footage, COPPA, VGGT's non-commercial weights, CPRA-sensitive receipts). Nothing on it blocks the hackathon. The document itself was later moved out of this repo (entry 29).
19. **Playable without the 3D.** "Make the game still playable without the 3D render?" → The scene ladder (UX.md): Cloud / Aisle / Strip drivers render one session identically — stops, COLLECT, scoring, and the receipt finish never depend on the renderer. Unmapped stores play in Strip by default; mapping becomes an invitation ("unlock the 3D view"), never a gate. A mid-hunt toggle drops a level for battery, comfort, or motion sensitivity. This also removes the launch cold-start problem: thousands of unmapped stores are playable on day one.
20. **The brain matches the game.** "Brain Arya's wireframes and UX needs to match the Quest ones. I like the Quest ones. Can we simplify Brain Arya's UI UX and build some wireframes before you develop?" → Brain wireframes join the same artifact in Quest's visual language: List (with a plain "why" per item), Add (five doors, Muse first), Ask, Receipts (review queue folded in as "Needs your eye" rows), Spend demoted from a tab to a card, and ONE unified Profile for both layers. Bottom bar: Today / List / Add / Ask / Profile — Quest lives as cards on Today, no Quest tab, no Dashboard tab. Process holds: wireframes first, his approval, then development.
21. **The thesis, in his words + the business.** Owner, excited: "Few things that I like: 1. Gamification — makes chore fun. 2. Like Google Maps, but for a grocery store. 3. Community based inventory reports. 4. It also helps keep track of grocery list, it's a smart grocery list maker (there are many, but how many make it fun?)" — then: "Marketing, promotions, monetization can be driven once we have virality." → BUSINESS.md: the four pillars; the three owned moments (list → shelf → receipt) as the position money follows; revenue in unlock order (retailer inventory data → shelf promotions per verified purchase → Arya Plus → platform licensing); density-beats-coverage and never-sell-honesty rules; competitive scan (Fetch/Ibotta, Shopkick shut down, Aisle411, first-party retailer apps, Cooklist, Simbe/Trax — every piece exists, the combination nowhere).
22. **One list, store lens.** "List should be per grocery store?" → Decided against separate lists: the need is household-level and patterns are store-blind, so the brain keeps ONE list; each item carries a store affinity learned from its receipts (the dictionary already tracks store-varying prices), and the List screen gains store chips (All / Costco / Trader Joe's) that lens the same items — store slice first, "could grab here too" below, check-off global, never duplicated. Hunts seed from the store's slice. Shown on the B1 wireframe in three switchable states per the owner's "yes, show it to me".
23. **Never charge the consumer.** Owner, correcting a draft that put analysis behind a paid "Plus" tier: "No — paid brain doesn't analyse. We never charge the consumer. Money will come from other sources, never the consumer." → BUSINESS.md amended: the consumer tier is deleted; the rule is stated as a principle (players are the supply; businesses pay). Analysis is a free product feature — Spend live, Patterns/Trends coming — and the wireframes carry no paid or premium language anywhere. Revenue stands at: retailer inventory data, brand promotions per verified purchase (rebate matching included), platform licensing.
24. **Friend run notifications.** "Can we notify friends of grocery runs?" → Yes (owner 👍): a run-start ping carries the store and the moment, never the list. Three structured actions, no chat: ⚔️ Challenge (the run is Shop-Off's starting gun), ➕ Add one thing (one item + qty as a request; the runner accepts/declines; accepted items file as bought-for-others — ledger yes, patterns never), 👁 Follow (progress count only). Household tier is warmer: direct list adds until the receipt closes the run. One ping per run, per-friend mute, mutual-accept friends only. Recipients are the owner's choice at two levels (his refinement): a standing notify set (bell toggle per friend in Friends) plus per-run chips at run start — set pre-selected, tap to add/drop for this run only. Household pings stay a separate tier (Profile-controlled, not per-run). On paper as panel 13b; engine work joins Stage C.
25. **One repo.** "Why is arya and arya quest 2 different repo?" — they were sequence, not architecture: arya (the product) came first; arya-quest was spun up at the pivot as a clean doc pack. Owner's call: "Merge the doc pack into the arya repo." → This doc pack now lives at vibhortayal/arya under docs/quest/; vibhortayal/arya-quest is archived with a pointer README. One product, one repo. (This entry is the last one written in the old home; the log continues in arya.)

Profile says 'Muse is the only way data enters Arya', but the Add tab shows five doors. I'd keep the in-app photo and voice doors in the demo account, since they would call the Meta API on camera. Voice should use Muse Voice Transcribe." → Profile's Muse-only claim is deleted; the invariant is restated as "every door runs on Meta models — Muse in chat, Spark on the Meta API in the app." Photo stays an in-app door (server decode via the Meta API — the /decode/photo path already exists); Voice is labeled Muse Voice Transcribe (build stage verifies the exact API surface; the live app's current device Web Speech is superseded by this ruling). This revises the 2026-10-27 "Muse-only intake" distribution lock for the demo account.

Build order: Intake parity first (already running — functional doors/voice in the same files), then the Brain restyle to B1–B6 on top of it, then test (suite + per-screen checks against the wireframes) and deploy for the owner's phone pass.

## Standing constraints the discussion produced

- No product build before wireframe approval (given 2026-10-27) and no submission without the owner's explicit go-ahead on the complete package.
- The full-3D scope rides on the VGGT spike verdict; hybrid and 2.5D fallbacks are pre-agreed in BUILD-PLAN.md.
- Fun is the data strategy; the receipt is always the finish.
30. **Add is one surface, not five doors (owner clarification + advisor synthesis, agreed).** Owner: Add was always meant for adding items to the LIST — voice, a photo of a handwritten list or whiteboard, paste from a WhatsApp message, typing, or via Spark. Receipts are a separate job: they enter by image only (app photo or via Spark), never by paste. Spark's synthesis, agreed by the owner: five door cards were build order, not user need — Type and Paste are one text field; "Via Muse" is a pointer line, not a card; one camera with smart routing (Spark can tell a list from a receipt by its totals; a receipt photographed in Add gets a one-tap "File it as a receipt", so the wrong door costs nothing and receipts still enter by image only). Add ships as: one text field (type/paste), one mic button, one camera button, one Muse line. Same turn, the owner's standing instruction: advise, don't transcribe — flag when a fragment conflicts with the whole picture before building it.
31. **Light mode only (owner).** "I don't like dark mode — use only light mode." → The Appearance switch is removed from Profile; the app always renders light. Also set: the wireframe-to-UI look-and-feel parity pass is a named stage after the current builds — screen-by-screen diff against the approved wireframes (Quest screens predate the Brain restyle's design pass), one polish stage, owner's phone pass as the gate.
32. **Collect celebration + Muse Image art (owner).** Muse Image is approved for the Aisle driver's art layer and the game's art generally (stylized surfaces, no truth claim). New Quest-mode moment, owner's design: when an item is COLLECTED at its shelf, the item appears as an animated character with one fun fact about the item — joy for the kid holding the phone, learning by association, Duolingo-style. Owner's boundary: fun facts only — NO nutritional or health claims. Guardrails (Spark): Quest mode only (Express stays uninterrupted); the moment is non-blocking (plays over the scene, dismisses itself, next stop already queued); characters are Muse Image stills animated by CSS motion (pop/bounce), pre-generated per item with a category-character fallback; fun facts ship as a small VERIFIED bank for the demo items — a model never improvises facts at kids live. Lands in the Oct 30–31 hunt-polish slot; driver-independent (overlays the hunt UI), so it does not wait on the Cloud decision.
33. **Motion navigation (owner ask, Spark design).** Owner: the view should move as the phone moves — gyroscope look-around, and the frames advancing as the user walks, instead of manual navigation. Split honestly: (a) gyro look-around ships — device orientation turns the view (cloud/aisle camera; photo frames pan left/right across the wide frame, which also spends the letterbox better); one-tap permission at hunt start (iOS gesture rule); manual navigation always remains — the judging demo is played seated, so movement can never be the only input. (b) Step-advance is an experiment flag: devicemotion step counting projects walked distance onto the path fraction u, and every COLLECT re-anchors u (collects are ground truth); manual navigation pauses it. (c) Camera-based live relocalization ("you are here" from what the lens sees) stays the rollout item it already was — not a Nov 4 feature; GPS is useless indoors at store scale.
34. **Wonder builds the localizer (owner commission).** Owner's correction of Spark's framing: Wonder's job is not runtime localization — it is AUTHORING the localization software. Commissioned as a spike (parallel, own workspace, app untouched): Wonder writes a feature-matching localizer over the 90 posed frames plus its own eval harness; held-out frames (half-second offsets, never indexed) score it — top-1 stop accuracy and median path error are the verdict numbers. Spark executes and reviews; the authorship trail (prompts/responses) is kept as evidence. If the number earns it, "Where am I?" (photo → nearest stop) becomes a demo feature; continuous tracking stays rollout.
35. **Quest is navigation, not a viewer (owner reframe).** Owner, correcting Spark's framing: gamification must make the shop FASTER. In-store, the player pushes a cart — navigating a video/scene by hand while grabbing items is too much friction. The intended experience: start Quest → the store's 3D layout is present → it moves as the cart moves (position from the localization stack: step-advance + collect anchoring + the Wonder-built matcher) → the player glances when they choose → near a list item, ONE quick action grabs/checks it → the app shows the guided path: next stop, route, run completion. Consequences: the hunt screen becomes navigation-first (auto-follow route, dominant next-stop card, route strip, one big GRAB for the current stop, celebration on grab); manual scene navigation is the preview/demo-explore mode and the fallback, not the primary loop; gyro look-around is demoted to garnish. The demo simulates the walk (judges are seated) — auto-advance IS the demo form of auto-follow. Real-store accuracy rides on the localization eval numbers when they land.
36. **Demo filmed in the store; hunt screen is the store (owner).** (a) The demo video will be filmed during a REAL shop in the store, Quest mode live — that is the impact shot. Judges play seated with screen navigation, which stays as the preview/judge mode. (b) Quest hunt screen = FULL-SCREEN PORTRAIT store view, nothing competing: top overlay is an "Up next" section (next stops in route order); when the player comes close to an item being bought, it POPS UP on screen (arrival pop with the big grab action); bottom shows route progress only. The navigation-first behaviors from Discussion 35 (auto-follow, guided route, one-tap grab, celebration on grab) are presented in this frame.
37. **Shelf Scan — the unmapped-store mode (owner).** Owner: for stores with no video/map, keep the camera on while shopping; the app captures shelf images, SAM 3.1 categorizes what is on the shelf against the shopper's LIST, and gives live help — a wall of 50 products overwhelms a person; SAM scans it at a glance. Design (Spark): frames sampled (view-change/on-pause, not raw continuous), SAM prompts built FROM the list with descriptive phrasings (per the item-box sample: bare nouns fail), Spark verifies candidate crops before anything pops (SAM proposes, Spark confirms — the house pattern), a verified item POPS with its box highlighted + the grab action (the Discussion 36 arrival pop, camera-fed). Confidence stays honest: weak categories (wrapped goods) say less. Strategic weight: this is the cold-start answer — mapped stores get navigation, unmapped stores get Shelf Scan, any store works day one. Scope: feasibility spike first (frames from the walkthrough, demo list); the demo slice is the pop on captured/live frames — the full viewfinder loop is post-freeze work. Frames are processed, not kept (bystanders — legal watchlist).
38. **Ray-Ban Meta: horizon only (owner).** Owner raised glasses for Shelf Scan (his original Grocery Navigator concept had them as the mapper). Spark's check: no public live-camera API for third-party apps — the working flow today is capture-on-glasses → sync to phone → ingest. Owner does not own a pair and ruled: "we can just use phone's camera for demo." → The demo runs on the phone camera throughout (Shelf Scan + in-store hunt). Glasses appear only as a named future surface in the pitch (camera where your eyes are, pops by audio) with no live-integration claim.
39. **Shelf Scan goes live-feed (owner).** Owner asked about livestreaming the phone camera; ruled "go ahead" on Spark's shape: NO raw video streaming (WebRTC is overkill — the model chain runs ~4–8s per frame, so transport is not the bottleneck). The scan view runs a live viewfinder and sends a frame when the view settles/changes (client-side differencing, 2–3s cadence bounds); verified pops overlay the live view; lag is shown, not hidden ("near-live" is the honest word). Hands-free end-state: chest-mounted phone + live feed + audio-free glance pops — the mount is a shopping errand, not a build.
40. **Filming kit: selfie stick (owner).** The hands-free mount question closed by the owner: he will use a selfie stick for the in-store filming and scanning. Mount search cancelled. The stick covers the demo's needs — raised shelf shots for Shelf Scan, over-cart POV for the video — with one hand on the cart.
41. **Finish takes only this trip's receipt — now or later (owner, 2026-10-27).** Owner, phone-testing the Finish screen, rejected the "Or use a receipt already filed" picker: "why would i need to tag an uploaded receipt." He is right: the receipt that settles a hunt must be THIS trip's receipt, snapped at finish — letting an old filed receipt settle any hunt breaks the receipt-as-proof rule the game stands on. The picker was an engine-stage test convenience that leaked to the screen; it is removed, and the client no longer sends an existing receipt_id at finish. The full rule: Finish offers "Snap the receipt" or a plain Skip. Skip finishes the hunt UNSETTLED: it is logged in the Receipts view as its own entry (store, date, items collected) with a small "Receipt missing" tag, and tapping the entry's "Upload the receipt" files this trip's photo through the normal decode, links it to this hunt, settles it, and clears the tag — still this trip's own receipt, attached late to its own hunt, never a picker over old receipts. While unsettled, the play record stands (stops, route, time) but receipt-verified rewards do not credit — no verified badges or board credit until the receipt lands (Spark's ruling from "the receipt is the proof," flagged for the owner's one-word veto).
42. **Receipt originals are kept and viewable in the app (owner, 2026-10-27).** Owner asked: "will my receipt images be available in the app?" The verified answer was no — decode kept the lines and discarded the photo. Now the filing path keeps the original: a receipt filed from an uploaded photo or PDF stores its bytes per account, exactly as uploaded, with its media type on the record; the receipt detail gains "View receipt" (the photo inline, the PDF at its own type). Receipts filed without an upload — the seeded history, order text — have no original and show no viewer; nothing is invented. The demo still files nothing. Storage is per-receipt files, not a media library: a receipt photo runs a few hundred KB against the existing 12 MB upload cap.
43. **Default view: photo-walk (owner, 2026-10-27); splat NO-GO per spike verdict — pose drift; parked until pose refinement.** The splat spike's verdict (~/workspace/arya-3d/splat-spike/VERDICT.md): gaussian splats beat the point cloud only at the entrance and collapse into smears at pasta/bread/frozen — the ceiling is VGGT pose quality, not training budget. The owner picked the photo-walk as the mapped store's default view: the walkthrough's own frames along the path, crossfaded by path fraction — real photos of the real store, honestly named (never a "3D model", never a live view). The point cloud stays one tap away as the alternate 3D view; Strip remains the unmapped default. The localizer's "Where am I?" gates on the store being mapped, not on a rung — position is a path fraction, so the anchor works on the Photo and Cloud views alike.
44. **Collect in any order (owner, 2026-10-27): the route is a recommendation, not a rail; celebration moved to a small corner card.** Owner, phone-testing a hunt: "I should be able to grab whatever I can grab — maybe the ordering is not correct, maybe the items have moved, or maybe I'm not following the recommended path." He is right: the hunt presented one stop at a time and only that stop could be collected. Now every stop is collectible from the hunt screen — each Up-next rail row carries its own GRAB, and tapping any stop's marker opens that stop's card with its GRAB; the arrival pop's big GRAB stays. After any grab, the hunt re-targets: the presented stop becomes the next uncollected stop after the one just grabbed, wrapping to the first uncollected when the route runs out; the walk, the step anchor, and step-advance follow the new presented stop. Sequential play is unchanged. Express was already any-order and is untouched. Same phone test: the collect celebration popped center-screen and blocked the store view. It is now a small card in the bottom-left corner above the route bar (character ≤56px, fact in the small size, max-width ~55% of the screen) — never over the center of the view, never over the Up-next rail. Timing and behavior are unchanged (Quest mode only, ~3.5s, tap dismisses, one at a time).
45. **Quest UI: wireframes first, again (owner, 2026-10-27 late).** Owner, phone-testing the built hunt the same night: the quest UI is "completely broken" — item markers arranged all over the screen, unchecking an item after GRAB breaks the UI, a finished hunt reads as stuck (the Today hero keeps it as an in-progress card; Skip's score screen reads as pending), Back from Finish dumps to Home, and Add-receipt routes through the Finish screen. The wireframes now live in this repo at `docs/quest/wireframes.html` (the Muse artifact remains the editing surface; the repo copy is the versioned reference). Quest UI code is frozen until the owner approves Hunt v2; the finish-flow presentation fixes built the same night are held on a branch, unshipped, and the rebuild — including uncheck-after-grab as an acceptance item — follows the approved wireframes.
46. **Hunt v2 approved and built (owner, 2026-10-28).** The owner approved the Hunt v2 wireframes in this repo and the amended rebuild plan ("implement"). Built on `fix/hunt-v2-rebuild`: the one-column hunt with one target in the store view; GRAB reversible in place ("↩ Put back", rows never move, the walk never restarts); no automatic route into Finish on any path (collect, Scan-shelf pop, resume — a finished hunt opens on its Score; Express completion is a toast); a tools row (Scan shelf, Where am I?, Not here?); the corner celebration carried over; Back is a stack. Finish closes the hunt: the closure Score, one label everywhere ("Finished · Receipt missing" / "Finished · Receipt attached ✓"), the Today hero clears, the hunt shows once under Finished, and Profile counts finished and receipt-attached hunts apart. Add receipt is a sheet over the invoking screen (the plan's allowed cut-line for 7b), reachable from Score, Today, Receipts and Profile. Copy never says "verified" for an attach — the server checks the merchant and the trip, not the haul. Server rules with it: a receipt settles one hunt only, and never one that began more than a day after the purchase date (#14); placeholder opponents are hidden from the list (#15); the demo's "Use a sample receipt" settles through the real attach path from a demo-only route. The 3D view follows the same one-target rule: its canvas sprites hide in the driver, since CSS cannot reach them.

The data purposes stand: community shortage reports (Spotter), store layout and shelf maps so people shop faster, consumption patterns and spend from receipts and list completion (with receipt management for returns), and fun with kids or a partner (split list, race). Built on `feat/game-shell`: install icons and manifest, hunt zoom lock, keyboard for the demo, then rewards — +10 per grab, milestone banners, a cosmetic hot streak, a full-haul burst, a Score that counts the haul up and banks it when the receipt lands (+50), and levels from banked points. The Quest HUD drops the clock for points; sound and buzz are opt-in. Game-flow wireframes (11 screens) are up for the owner's review.

## 54. Shares, wild finds, and the player (owner, 2026-10-28)

Owner's virality pass, decided in chat in three moves: build all
three Score shares; encourage haul photos so Arya gets the data
and identifies the products; and answer the impulse-buy question
with a system, not a scold. Shipped as one PR on top of phone
pass 2.

- **The player.** One cast member fronts your cards: pick in
  Profile (23 portraits from the registry), stored per account
  (POST /api/quest/avatar, validated against the portraits on
  disk; profile() carries it). Default: the basket.
- **Haul card.** Settled Score → Share your haul: a canvas card
  (1080x1350) hosted by your player — the hunt's items pop in as
  their characters, points count up, the haul photo (if snapped)
  fronts it. The animation records to a short video where the
  browser allows (captureStream + MediaRecorder), else a still.
  Native share sheet only; nothing auto-posts; spend never
  appears on a card (pinned by test).
- **Love note.** Pick a grabbed item; its character fronts a
  "Found & loved" card; the caption tags the chain (@traderjoes,
  @costco, ... from a small map in share.js).
- **Shelf SOS.** Pick from THIS hunt's not-here reports; the
  card's trip count comes from GET /api/quest/sos-count — the
  player's own reports for that store+item across distinct
  sessions, nothing else. "3 trips in a row" prints only when
  the reports prove 3.
- **Snap your haul.** POST /api/quest/haul-photo runs the Eyes
  two-read detect and matches sightings to the hunt (token
  Jaccard >= 0.5 — 'Oat Milk Chocolate' is not 'Milk'). Seen,
  extras, not-seen; not-seen never claims an absence. The read
  saves on the session; the photo is not kept. Extras get a
  one-tap add-to-list.
- **Wild finds.** Impulse buys, caught three ways: declared
  live (+ Wild find in the hunt tools — joins collected, flagged
  wild), caught by the receipt (session_view diffs settled
  receipt lines against hunt items), caught by the camera
  (haul-photo extras). The Score wears a Wild finds strip; the
  haul card names up to three. Never framed as failure — they
  are the most shareable part of the haul. The third layer —
  promoting repeat wild finds into plan usuals with a label —
  is designed, not built (needs the list-vs-receipt join).
