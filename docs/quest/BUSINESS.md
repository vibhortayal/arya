# Who it is for, and who else is out there

Companion to [`PITCH.md`](../archive/quest/PITCH.md) (the story, archived on 2026-11-04) and [`docs/PRINCIPLES.md`](../PRINCIPLES.md) (why Arya exists). Originally written 2026-10-27; reworked 2026-11-02. The goal is that people use Arya and help each other.

## Who it is for

Shoppers: the people who keep a household or a group fed. First parents and households, who feel the problem most; then anyone who shares a list. Built and tested with US receipts, dollars and stores first; other markets not tested.

## The four pillars (my words, 2026-10-27)

1. **Gamification**: it makes a chore fun.
2. **Google Maps, but for a grocery store**: the indoor map layer.
3. **Community-based inventory reports**: shelf truth from the players.
4. **A smart grocery list maker**: "there are many, but how many make it fun?"

Fun brings people in. The map is what they build together. Community reports keep it true. The smart list keeps them coming back. Each pillar makes the next one possible.

## The community side

Arya works better the more shoppers help each other, and it asks for nothing in return:

- **Lists and groups.** A household or a group shares lists, so everyone sees the same thing.
- **Store maps.** Maps come from shoppers' own guided scans; people are pixelated before anything is kept. The first person to scan a store starts its map.

Two rules keep that trustworthy:

- **Density beats coverage.** A thin layer over a thousand stores helps nobody. Win one neighbourhood's stores completely, then grow.
- **Honesty is never traded away.** Receipt-verified results are what people trust. A faked location, a buyable badge or a sold receipt would destroy that. Personal data is never sold, and there are no ads or sponsored placements in Quest.

## Competitive landscape (checked 2026-10-27)

This table is the Quest-era view. The fuller desk research of 2026-11-02, with
every outside claim linked or marked unverified, is [`docs/MARKET.md`](../MARKET.md).

Every piece of Arya Quest exists somewhere. The combination exists nowhere.

| Player | What they have | What they lack |
|---|---|---|
| **Fetch / Ibotta** | Receipt scanning as a points game; brand-funded offers; scale | No map, no hunt, no list brain, no store truth. Points for receipts, not play |
| **Shopkick** (shut down) | Proved shoppers will play in-store for rewards; brand-funded kicks | Paid-errand economics failed. Rewards were cash-like costs, not status; no map, no inventory, no brain |
| **Aisle411** | Indoor item location; retailer-licensed 2D maps (Walgreens, Marsh Supermarkets; largely dormant since the mid-2010s) | B2B only, built on retailer-supplied data. No game, no community, no freshness from play, no independent coverage of stores that publish nothing |
| **Walmart / Target apps** | Aisle locations in their own stores | First-party only. Each covers its own chain, from its own planogram. Nothing cross-store, nothing for Trader Joe's, independents, or ethnic markets |
| **Cooklist** | Loyalty-connected pantry and list generation ("Mint for groceries") | No game, no map, no store-side data. A brain without a body |
| **Simbe / Trax** | Shelf truth for retailers, via robots and auditors | B2B cost structure (robots, field staff). No consumer layer, no game, no lists |
| **AnyList / list apps** | The list | Starts blank, knows no receipts, no stores, no play |

The wedge: stores that publish nothing (Trader Joe's, independents, ethnic markets) are unmapped by every incumbent, because incumbents need retailer data or retailer permission. Arya Quest needs neither. Its players are the sensors, and the game is why they show up.
